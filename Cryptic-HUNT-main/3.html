01 Overview — What This File Is

On 8 March 2014 a Boeing 777-200ER, registration 9M-MRO, left Kuala Lumpur for Beijing as Malaysia Airlines Flight 370, carrying 239 people. Less than an hour into the flight it stopped answering air traffic control. It was then tracked by military radar back across the Malay Peninsula, and afterwards only by the faint electronic handshakes it kept exchanging with a satellite.

Read carefully: a theory is not a finding. Investigators have established where the flight most likely ended; none has established why. This file keeps the two apart — and so should you.

The aircraft has not been found. Most of what is known comes from two final signals: a voice, and a satellite. Both are printed under Last Signals. Read them slowly — and mind the clock they were recorded on.

02 Parties

Captain Zaharie Ahmad Shah Pilot in command

Aged 53, with decades of flying experience. His reply to Kuala Lumpur control was the last voice contact. The 2018 safety report could not determine why the flight left its route.

Fariq Abdul Hamid First Officer

Aged 27, flying a line-training sector. One of twelve crew aboard, alongside 227 passengers.

Kuala Lumpur Area Control Ground · last voice contact

Told the crew to contact Ho Chi Minh control. The reply was heard; the handover never completed.

Inmarsat Satellite operator

Its Indian Ocean Region satellite logged the aircraft's automatic handshakes. Analysis of their timing is the basis for the search area.

Ocean Infinity Seabed search contractor

Searched in 2018 and again from March 2025 under a "no find, no fee" contract with Malaysia. Paid only if major wreckage is found.

Voice 370 Families' group

Represents relatives of those aboard. Has pressed Malaysia to keep the search going and to widen it to other contractors.

03 Timeline

All clock times in this timeline are Malaysia Time (MYT), UTC+8, the clock the cockpit and the controllers used.

  1. 00:41 Flight 370 takes off from Kuala Lumpur, 8 March 2014.
  2. 01:07 Crew reports cruising at flight level 350.
  3. 01:19 ★ Last voice contact (see Last Signals).
  4. 01:21 Transponder stops near waypoint IGARI, at the edge of Malaysian and Vietnamese airspace.
  5. 01:38 Ho Chi Minh control asks Kuala Lumpur where the flight is.
  6. 02:22 Last military primary radar contact, over the Andaman Sea.
  7. 02:25 Satellite link comes back; the numbered handshakes begin.
  8. 07:24 Malaysia Airlines states that contact has been lost.
  9. 08:10 Final ground-initiated handshake answered.
  10. 08:19 ★ Last satellite handshake (see Last Signals).
  11. 24 Mar 2014 Satellite analysis places the end of the flight in the southern Indian Ocean.
  12. 29 Jul 2015 A flaperon washes up on Réunion.
  13. Jan 2017 The government-led underwater search is suspended.
  14. Jul 2018 Malaysia's safety report cannot determine the cause.
  15. Mar 2025 – Jan 2026 Ocean Infinity's renewed search finds nothing.

04 Evidence Locker

Some exhibits below have been partially declassified. Others haven't — yet. Some blacked-out lines respond better to a mouse than to your eyes. Try dragging your cursor across one.

Exhibit A — Réunion Flaperon Found 29 Jul 2015

A wing-trailing-edge part, washed ashore about 4,000 km west of the search area. Identified as belonging to 9M-MRO. Its drift is consistent with the Indian Ocean search zone.

Exhibit B — Handshake Log Mar 2014

Seven exchanges between the aircraft's satellite terminal and the ground station. Each gives a distance to the satellite, not a position, so each defines an arc.
